PERMANENT SINGLE-PROTOCOL MATHEMATICAL DRAWING CONTRACT

This contract applies to every future online-course lecture. Diagram generation is
optional, never a quota. Create a figure only when omitting it would materially
impair understanding of a spatial, geometric, incidence, quotient, gluing, or
multi-map relationship that prose and displayed formulas cannot communicate as
clearly. Zero figures is a normal successful result.

1. PURPOSE AND DIAGRAM-TYPE GATE

Before writing TikZ, privately complete the sentence:

    This figure shows that ...

The sentence must identify the precise mathematical relationship the reader should
understand. If it cannot be stated clearly, omit the figure. Use a map or
commutative diagram when the essential information concerns spaces and maps. Use a
spatial or geometric diagram only when the essential information is genuinely
geometric or topological. Do not force an abstract map relation into a misleading
picture of lines or regions.

2. OBJECT-TYPE FIDELITY

Every drawn object must look like the mathematical type it represents.

- A two-dimensional open set or neighborhood must be an actual region, not one arc.
- A boundary must be a boundary curve or hyperplane, not the region it bounds.
- A point must be visibly a point.
- An overlap must visibly lie in both relevant regions.
- A Euclidean neighborhood that crosses a boundary hyperplane must visibly cross
  that hyperplane, including the portion outside a relative half-space domain.
- Arrows, projections, inclusions, restrictions, and compositions must have the
  correct sources, targets, and directions.

Labels alone never repair a wrong visual type. If faithful representation would be
ambiguous or misleading, use a map diagram or omit the figure.

3. THE ONLY AUTHORING FORM

Every new non-textbook figure must use exactly one backend and source form:

    backend=tikz

Its source must contain exactly one complete body-local `tikzpicture`. Penrose,
Quiver/tikz-cd, Asymptote, Graphviz, CeTZ, raw `\includegraphics`, and an unwrapped
`tikzpicture` are forbidden. Do not emit a preamble, `\usepackage`, command
definitions, file operations, URLs, or absolute paths.

Use only this LaTeX-safe source-block protocol, with every source line commented:

% MPB-DIAGRAM-SOURCE-BEGIN: diagram-0123456789abcdef backend=tikz
%| \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=1cm]
%|   \draw[->] (0,0) -- (2,0) node[right] {$V$};
%| \end{tikzpicture}
% MPB-DIAGRAM-SOURCE-END: diagram-0123456789abcdef

Preserve the assigned diagram id. The application compiles, verifies, caches, and
inserts the vector PDF. Never emit `\includegraphics` or a generated path yourself.

4. SEMANTIC AND VISUAL ACCEPTANCE

Check every object, label, arrow direction, region, containment, incidence,
exclusion, and panel relationship against the evidence. Check label separation,
crossings, clipping, scale, mathematical typography, whitespace, and reading order.
All visible text must be English and all notation must be real LaTeX. Keyframes,
board screenshots, raster previews, and page renders remain evidence only.

The figure must be introduced or explained by nearby prose. If prose says "the
following diagram" or makes another forward figure reference, the diagram must
actually follow. If no figure is emitted, remove every such forward reference. Do
not leave an orphan figure whose mathematical role is never explained. If the
evidence is insufficient or prose is equally clear, omit the figure.

5. PIPELINE AND TEXTBOOK FIGURES

Only an included whole-lecture candidate receives `backend=tikz` and source. The
renderer is the compilation authority; rejected source is never a successful
preview. The canonical deliverable and application preview are the same standalone
vector PDF.

For this textbook-led course, include the corresponding original textbook figures.
For every adopted passage in the primary AND reference books, inspect its original
page and continuation pages and include its associated diagrams. A diagram from
another book with similar meaning is not grounds for omitting it. Explain changed
notation beside each original. An identical original already included earlier may
be explicitly cross-referenced. Do not pad the lecture with diagrams of unadopted
exercises or later topics. Record each included source page, asset hash and placement
in SOURCES.json; record concrete exclusions in the reading notes.
Prefer an original figure over a newly drawn imitation. If the assigned primary
pages have no figure, do not invent one; use a relevant supplementary original
with an explicit attribution when it helps explain the mathematics.
An indexed original may be a native-vector PDF or an original PDF crop carrying
verified source-PDF hash, physical page, clipping rectangle and crop hash:

% MPB-TEXTBOOK-FIGURE: sha256=<64 lowercase hexadecimal digits> width=0.82

Run `course check` (one subsection) or `course episode-check` before importing. Its
`figures.needs_attention` lists, for every original, the printed size in mm, the scale
relative to the book and any isolated stray mark (scan dirt) with its position. When a
listed mark is not mathematical content, append ` clean=marks` to that marker line:

% MPB-TEXTBOOK-FIGURE: sha256=<...> width=0.82 clean=marks

The importer then covers exactly those marks in white while typesetting; the original
file and its hash are unchanged. Leave a mark that is content (a node dot, a period of
a formula) uncovered. A warning that the letters can only print below 1.8 mm means the figure is too wide
or too tall for the page: split it or crop it tighter instead of accepting it.

The width is a maximum, never a demand to fill that fraction of the line. The importer
sizes every original itself: books are scanned at different scales, so it measures the
labels of all indexed figures of the same source book and scales that book's figures
alike, so that their main labels print about as tall as a capital of the body text
(2.5 mm), never wider than 70% of the line or taller than a page-height limit. Do not
try to compensate with the width value. Check
the actual formal PDF for readable labels, balanced adjacent diagrams, whitespace
and page breaks after import; a successful compilation alone is insufficient.
Keep a short explanation and its diagram together, for example in a small minipage
inside a standard center block, preserving ordinary separation between consecutive
groups. Allow the surrounding long theorem or proof to break normally across pages.

The application copies the indexed bytes unchanged and rechecks the hash. A
verified original crop preserves source raster pixels as well as vector artwork;
do not resample, redraw, artificially sharpen or claim extra detail. A scanned
source cannot acquire more information through a higher export DPI. Preserve
original labels and give an English caption or label key outside the crop.
Unindexed images, arbitrary screenshots and unverified raster artwork are rejected.
